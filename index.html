Learn Communication with Phoebe

Six sessions, 45 minutes each, and one message you rebuild in every single one of them. Not communication theory and not a personality course. The mechanics: working out what you actually want the person to do, hearing them before you draft, putting the ask where they will see it, and saying the hard version out loud. In session 4 a scorer reads your real text and tells you which sentence your ask is buried in, which is a harder audience than any colleague.

The six sessions 💬 · one message, rebuilt in all of them

You are Daybreak's data lead and the subscription redesign is three weeks late. You have to tell the founder. That is the message. It is currently 163 words, it contains nine corporate abstractions, and the thing you want from them is in the last sentence, which is why the last three updates got no reply.

🎯 Session 1 · easy

The ask

Who is reading it, what you want them to actually do, and the one-sentence test. Most updates that get no reply contain no ask at all, and their authors have never noticed.

▶ Start here 🎧 Session 2 · easy

Listening first

You cannot structure a message for somebody you have not heard. The two questions to ask before drafting, what a non-reply is telling you, and reflecting back as a correction mechanism.

before you draft 🧭 Session 3 · medium

Structure

The ask goes first. Three skeletons worth knowing, the buried-lede failure with a number on it, and why the same structure survives an email, a document and a room.

ask first 📝 Session 4 · medium

The written version

The scorer. Sentence length, passive voice, abstraction, specifics, and ask position - all measured on your real text. Take the message from 5 to 100, then switch on the lever everybody asks for.

★ the scorer 🎤 Session 5 · hands-on

The spoken version

The same skeleton out loud. The opening line, what has to be cut, why the interruption is usually the real question, and using silence on purpose.

the room 🪨 Session 6 · hands-on

Bad news and disagreement

Delivering it upward early and specifically, disagreeing without damage, taking the pushback without defending, and the repair conversation nobody teaches.

What is honest here 🧾

The scorer is mostly measurement and partly a rubric, and it says which is which on its own footer.

ClaimStatus
Where your ask is, sentence N of MReally measured on your own text. It finds the first sentence that asks for a decision or an action and reports its position
Words per sentence, specifics, total lengthReally measured. Plain string analysis, no model involved
Passive-voice countMeasured by a regex heuristic over a fixed participle list. It will miss some and occasionally flag a false one. The page says so where the number appears
Abstraction countMeasured against a list of 30 entries, chosen not derived. A starting point for noticing, not a standard
The score out of 100A stated rubric weighting those six measurements. Two people can write a 70 and an 85 and the 70 can be the better message for its reader
Daybreak, the founder, the messageA teaching scenario. The measurement code is real; the situation is written
